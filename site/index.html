When the sun sets, the duck stands up

At midday, solar power floods California's grid and the demand left for other sources (net demand) sinks into the duck's belly. At sunset it surges back into the neck. Here's tomorrow's curve, forecast from open data and scored against the grid operator's own forecast.

Method

Forecast. One gradient-boosted model per target (gross demand, and net demand = demand − solar − wind) at hourly resolution. Inputs:

  • Hour, weekday, California holidays, season and a slow trend.
  • Temperature at six load centres, plus the previous day's inland peak heat.
  • Solar radiation and cloud over the Mojave and Central Valley, and wind at the Tehachapi–Mojave wind belt.
  • Load at the same hour two and seven days earlier, which is all that's known when a day-ahead forecast is made.

The model is trained January 2020 to June 2025 and tested from July 2025, and the 80% bands come from test errors by hour.

Data. CAISO "Today's Outlook" five-minute history, averaged to the hour. Weather is from ERA5 reanalysis for training and the forecast for tomorrow (Open-Meteo).

Honest limitations

  • Training on reanalysis weather but forecasting with forecast weather flatters the test score a little. Real-time errors will be somewhat larger.
  • CAISO's forecast covers a slightly different footprint and is built with far more inputs, including behind-the-meter solar estimates, so the comparison shows how close public data gets, not a like-for-like contest.
  • Net demand here subtracts only grid-scale solar and wind that CAISO reports. Rooftop solar shows up as lower demand.
  • "Today's Outlook" is CAISO's preliminary operational data, revised later in its settled datasets.
